Kati? App
Problem Statement
I was thinking, what can I get for 500 rupees today? Turns out lots of things, from meat masala, to toothpaste, to small toys to other essentials.
I want a simple way to see what my money can realistically buy around me, based on actual local prices and everyday experiences. 
What is it?
Kati? The app is basically  “What Can I Buy With Rs. X?”, meaning a  city specific app that helps people understand the real-world value of their money in Kathmandu in different places. 
The prices and experiences are crowdsourced from local users, Meaning the developer will make surveys, it can be mouth to mouth or simply asking shopkeepers the price or creating a survey and using platforms like reddit nepal to ask for users to find the price , making the information more relevant to each area.
Objective
The main objective is to make money easier to understand in everyday terms.
The app aims to:
Show users what their money can realistically buy.
Provide local and area-specific price information.
Let people contribute and update prices from their own experiences.(the developer will keep this in future update)
Limitations
As I thought, I realised that if I don't always go I cannot maintain the price. It can be changed and if i cannot provide hard fact, people may lose trust in app
There are many things, so I will only focus on essentials like hygiene, food, tech etc. Not anymore, because I have to go by myself .

Mitigation
I will also put place where user can also suggest prices, it needs fact checking but if 2 user went to same store and found same price, it will be added
I will try to contact shopkeepers to add the app and add their own price, giving brief tutorials on how to do that. This will create the most fact check. 
Initial Focus
The first version will focus on Kathmandu, starting with the Ghattekulo Area.
Users will enter an amount, such as Rs. 500, Rs. 1,000, or Rs. 5,000, and the app will show what that amount can buy locally across categories such as food, transportation, entertainment, fuel, coffee, and mobile data.
As more people contribute prices, the platform can expand from small things to huge data driven things cities across Nepal.
